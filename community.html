Tab 05

Community & Content

This is not a miscellaneous tab. It is a second line of work I keep returning to: understanding a player or learner community, organising information for it, making content, and changing that content based on what comes back.

Selected Work

  1. 01

    NetEase Blizzard China Gaming Content Creator

    Creator Partnership · Digital Media / Gaming Content Creator · 2024–Present

    How I workI make game content for Chinese audiences, then use the response, views, saves, comments and community behaviour, to decide what is worth making next.

    The work covers Hearthstone and broader game content, cosplay, livestream formats, and short-form video. I handle the full production loop on most of it: choosing the topic, recording, editing, publishing, and then deciding what the next one should be.

    A separate strand is localisation. I translate and adapt game-design and developer talks for Bilibili, so that developer perspectives and interactive-media concepts reach Chinese-speaking audiences who would not otherwise see them. That is knowledge translation rather than reaction content, and it is the part I find most useful to my design work.

    The loop I actually run on each piece is: why this topic, what the hook and format are, how it was produced, what the public response looked like, and what I changed on the next one because of it.

    Audience
    20K+ Douyin followers.
    Platforms
    Douyin and Bilibili, short-form video and longer localised talks.

  2. 02

    HEYBOX Rainbow Six Wiki & Player Community

    HEYBOX / Rainbow Six Siege community · Community & Wiki Contributor

    The actual problemNot “write encyclopedia entries.” Decide how complex, constantly changing game knowledge should be structured so players can find it, read it, and keep it current.

    Rainbow Six Siege carries a large amount of detail that matters in play: operators, gadgets, map geometry, counters, and the way all of it shifts with each balance change. Players do not arrive wanting to read a page. They arrive with a specific question, usually mid-frustration.

    So the work was information design for a live game. I looked at what players were actually trying to find and could not, worked out how an entry should be structured to answer that quickly, kept the structure consistent from entry to entry so the second page someone reads behaves like the first, and maintained entries as the game changed underneath them.

    Treating a wiki as a design surface rather than as writing is the part that transfers. Consistency across entries is a system decision, and deciding what to leave out is most of the work.

  3. 03

    Schoolive Education Community & Moodle Platform

    Schoolive Interactive Ltd. · Co-founder / Creative Director · 2022–Present

    The actual problemThe hard part was never Moodle. It was deciding how learning content should be structured, surfaced, and kept useful.

    Schoolive is a free online education platform providing psychology, game design and interactive media resources for Chinese-speaking students. I co-founded it and I am responsible for its creative direction.

    My work is content architecture rather than platform administration: how courses are organised, what a learning path looks like from a beginner's first session to a later one, which material should be published, which should be updated, and which should be reorganised because the current shape is not working.

    Learner feedback is what drives most of those decisions. When people stall in the same place, that is usually a sequencing problem rather than a content gap, and the fix is structural.

    The same platform also carries an educational game project whose creative direction I lead. That work sits under Game Design.
