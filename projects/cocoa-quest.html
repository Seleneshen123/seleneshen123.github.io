Cocoa Quest

A biodesign project about cocoa: what its by-products can become, how chocolate is made, and the ethical and environmental issues along the supply chain behind it.

Overview

Cocoa Quest is a team project for ARTG 5000, Biodesign. It looks at cocoa from several directions at once: how chocolate is produced, what cocoa by-products can become as biomaterials, how packaging could be more sustainable, and the ethical and environmental problems inside the cocoa supply chain.

The project has moved through two prototypes so far. The first was an educational chocolate-making kit in biomaterial packaging. The second, now in development, is an educational tabletop game. The page follows that order.

My Contribution

  • Conducting the biomaterial experiments
  • Comparing material formulations
  • Testing and iterating the prototypes
  • Designing the educational tabletop game direction
  • Developing gameplay ideas that connect cocoa production, child labor, sustainability, and material reuse

Cocoa Quest is a team project. Where a piece of work on this page was made by a teammate, it is credited as such.

Prototype 1 — Biomaterial Packaging

Our first prototype, now complete, was an educational chocolate-making kit with biomaterial packaging. The packaging explored a gelatin-based bioplastic composite with ingredients such as glycerol, kaolin clay, coconut coir, and cacao nibs. A wax coating was explored for the inside.

Design

The kit was planned as a cocoa-shell-shaped case holding everything needed to make chocolate, with the packaging built from biomaterial panels over a folded board.

Physical prototype

The finished package, opened. The composite is a dark sheet with the coir fibres and cacao nibs visible in it.

Instructions

Two illustrated sheets were drafted for the prototype kit. The first sets out safety rules, the kit contents, and six steps for making the chocolate, marking which steps are for an adult and which for a child. The second adds product information, simple cocoa facts, and a bonus quest for reusing the shell.

Both are prototype concept materials, not final commercial packaging. Some fields were never filled in, such as “Best before: xxxx”.

Note

The composite and its wax coating were material experiments. No performance or sustainability results are claimed for them here.

Design Iteration

After feedback and further discussion, our team decided that the next prototype should put more weight on learning through gameplay.

The aim is not simply to tell players that chocolate production has ethical and environmental problems. We want them to face meaningful decisions, and through those decisions understand the cocoa supply chain, what production costs, the conditions people work under, and the environmental consequences.

A short story comic about child labor in cocoa production became supporting material for that shift.

Prototype 2 — Educational Tabletop Game

Status

In development. The game is not finished, and nothing below is a final rule, a completed component, or a playtest result.

We are now designing a tabletop game made with bio-friendly materials. It explores cocoa production, economic choices, labor ethics, and sustainability through decisions the players make.

One of my earlier gameplay ideas explores the trade-off between hiring cheaper child labor and employing adult workers. The point is to let players feel how economic incentives can push towards exploitative labor, and why a short-term cost advantage can carry serious social consequences.

We want the mechanics to follow the actual chocolate production process and the life cycle of the materials. We are also exploring making the game’s physical components from biomaterials, possibly including waste from cocoa itself.
